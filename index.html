Part I

Executive Memo

Valuation Conclusion

Primary Method (GGM): Intrinsic value of $385/share — a 23% discount to the June 2025 market price of $497. On this basis, Microsoft is OVERVALUED at the valuation date. The Exit Multiple method ($574) is presented as a cross-check only, anchoring to current market pricing of mature cash flows rather than long-run fundamental value.

Core Thesis

The thesis is a timing bet, not a quality bet. Microsoft's business quality is not in question — EBIT margins have expanded from 41.6% in FY2021 to 45.6% in FY2025, and the company generates over $136B in annual operating cash flow. The valuation question is whether the market correctly prices the duration and magnitude of the current CapEx suppression cycle.

The base case models CapEx normalisation from 22% of revenue in FY2026 to 12.5% by FY2030, consistent with Microsoft's pre-AI historical profile (FY2022: 12.0%, FY2023: 13.3%). This drives FCFF expansion from $83B to $172B — a 107% increase over five years and the mechanical engine of the bull thesis for long-term holders.

Key Observations

  1. Terminal growth rate is the dominant variable — a 200bps move in g at base-case WACC produces a $135/share range ($331–$466).
  2. WACC is the second most sensitive input — a 200bps increase at base-case g of 3.0% compresses implied price by $162/share ($482 → $320).
  3. The $497 market price requires a terminal growth rate above 4.0% under the WACC scenario for the base case — confirming the overvaluation finding is robust.
  4. The exit multiple cross-check at 20× implies $574, above the entire GGM base-case WACC row, reflecting market pricing of AI optionality not captured in the GGM's normalised cash flow assumption.

Insights & Skills

Key Analytical Insights

CapEx cycle is the valuation swing factor

The difference between bear ($222) and bull ($494) GGM implied prices is driven almost entirely by CapEx assumptions. At 22% through FY2030, FCF margin stays suppressed at ~26%. At 12.5% (base), FCF margin recovers to 36%+, consistent with Microsoft's pre-AI cash generation profile.

The market requires an unrealistic terminal growth assumption to justify $497

Justifying the current market price requires g above 4.0% under any plausible WACC — well above long-run US nominal GDP growth, leaving no margin for error.

Exit multiple is structurally above GGM in this environment

The $189 gap between $574 (exit multiple) and $385 (GGM) represents the market's implicit premium for AI optionality — the portion of current pricing that cannot be explained by normalised long-run cash flows.

Post-valuation price action directionally validates the model

The ~10% decline from $497 to ~$450 between June 2025 and May 2026 is consistent with the overvaluation finding. The GGM verdict of −23% implied more correction than has occurred, but the directional signal was correct.
